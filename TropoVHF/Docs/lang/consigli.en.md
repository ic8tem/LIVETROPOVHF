# Tips for the operator

> Automatic translation by Italian: in case of doubt is worth the Italian guide.

Practical indications to use the tab Forecasts in station work. They do not replace listening: The forecast says where it's best to look, the radio says if it's there.

## Before starting

1. Write your landlord to 8 or 10 characters: so the soil share comes from the soil model Copernicus GLO-30 and just the antenna height from the ground. With 4 or 6 characters the quota of the antenna on the sea is indispensable.
2. Measure the actual length of the boom and the loss of the cable to the frequency used: Two dBs of cable are as good as a shorter antenna.
3. Choose the environment of the receiver (city, residential, rural, quiet rural). In the city the artificial noise at 144 MHz can exceed much that of the receiver: is often the first limit.
4. Let the calculation log on: If a result seems strange, there is reason.

## Read the map

- Look at the colors first, then the lines. Yellow means strong layer but no duct for the band; Red and purple mean led.
- Change band at the same time: often the conduit is at 432 or 1296 MHz and not at 144 MHz, because the 2 m need a conduit more often.
- Sliding hours: the ground ducts grow at night and at dawn and break in the afternoon with the sun and the wind. Anticyclone on the sea also lasts days.
- The isobares show where the anticyclone stands; the curves of the reverse where the air at altitude is warmer. If the gain follows the inversion more than the pressure, count the layer close to the ground.
- The Alps and the Apennines interrupt the low conduits: a colored area that ends up against a mountain range usually does not cross it.

## Weather situations that count

From the experience of ARRL Handbook (§ 19.4.3) and NBS studies:

- Slow anticyclones: the air that drops heats up and forms inversions between 500 and 3000 m. They are the most extensive ducts and last even days. In the northern hemisphere the best routes are south of the center of high pressure.
- Evening and morning: the ducts strengthen when the soil cools; the inversions at sunrise and sunset are worth up to 20 dB more.
- Radiation inversions (slow and calm lands, dry air, valleys): they improve the signal but rarely do a true conduit.
- In front of a warm front, for a few hundred km, the conditions are often good; behind a cold forehead the reverse is strong but unstable, and it is better to look parallel to the forehead.
- Wind of land along the coasts after sunset: fresh air on the ground and hot at 200–300 m, also stretched over 800 km along the coast.
- Foehn (also in the Alps): hot and dry air that drops over cold air or snowy soil.
- Scirocco: hot and dry air from the Sahara over the freshest and wet Mediterranean; Strong inversions, which gave records over 1500 km in microwave.
- Temporals, cold fronts and strong wind break the layers.

## Before a link

1. Place the corresponding locator (clicking on the map or writing it) and calculate the signal.
2. Read the founding layer: continuity on the route, covered ground, ground inside the layer. A conduit on the sea that does not arrive above the land of the correspondent serves little.
3. Look if the antennas are inside, above or below the conduit: from a antenna much above the top the signal enters badly.
4. Compare the S/N with the threshold of the way: if in SSB not enough, FT8 can suffice with 20 dB less.
5. If the forecast is good but the treat silent, try to point a little to the side: the duct can be moved to the maximum circle.
6. If the route crosses a mountainous island (Sardinia, Corsica), try some degree towards the stretch of free sea, for example the Bocche di Bonifacio; with strong wind in the narrow lane makes little. In depth in the forecast manual, § 10 quinquies.

## During the opening

- Note now UTC, name, locator, band, way and report: is the data you need to check the model.
- Check high band beacons: are the fastest way to realize that the duct is there.
- On the routes that cross the mountains do not discard the afternoon of summer: if the layer is removed up to the share of the common volume, the troposcatter is strengthened (strong signs but with rapid evanescence). The calculation signals it with the line «Troposcatter afternoon». Forecast manual, § 8 ter.
- On FT8 and in clusters the program collects ads and decodings from itself; for 432 and 1296 MHz operator reports remain essential.

## Limits to be held

> 'Prohibition cannot be generalized, but studied in relation to local morphology.' — DF5AI, reported by IK3XTV

The map says where conditions are favourable; for its route counts the calculation point-point on the real ground.

- The GFS has hundreds of metres apart: thin inversions are estimated, not seen (double calculation A and B). On the ground the GFS sees about half of the ducts of a finer model.
- The forecast is more reliable in the first 24–48 hours; beyond is a trend indication.
- Signal values are used to compare situations. A discard of 5–10 dB compared to the real is normal.
- Temporals, cold fronts and strong wind break the layers before the pattern anticipates.
